01

Build the receipt bar and put it on everything

One idea, first, because every other decision follows from it. Ground News owns bias. Nobody owns corroboration. Your pipeline already verifies stories across sources — then throws that away into grey 12px text reading "Reuters, BBC +2."

That line is the most valuable thing on the canvas and it's set smaller than the date. Turn it into a designed object that appears on every asset you publish, at three sizes, animated in the reels. A strip of ticks — one per independent outlet — that fills as corroboration strengthens, with the count set large and the three highest-weight outlets named underneath.

A screenshot of your post should argue for the app without a word of marketing copy in it.

Why this is the bet

  • It can't be faked. Generating it honestly requires a multi-source verification pipeline. Competitors without one can't copy it; competitors who fake it get caught once, publicly.
  • It kills the empty-canvas problem structurally. Every slide now carries a piece of real information design, so no slide can be pure type on a gradient again.
  • It's the trust position, made visible. Trust in news sits at a record-low 37% and 62% of people actively worry about telling real from fake. That's a market asking for exactly this.
  • It travels. Anyone reposting or screenshotting your card carries the bar with them.

The rule that makes it work

A single-source story must render as a visibly thin, outlined, one-tick bar — and you have to be willing to post it that way. The moment it's padded to look impressive it becomes a liability instead of an asset. Build and test that degraded case first, so it can never be quietly skipped later.

02

Two surfaces, and colour that survives a thumbnail

Your brand is warm, editorial and terracotta. You've rendered it as near-black at low internal contrast, so every post is the same dark hole in the grid. Paper becomes the default; ink becomes the exception.

Alternating surfaces by slot means the profile grid checkerboards instead of smearing. That single change does more for feed presence than any amount of typographic refinement, because presence is a function of contrast at the edge of the post — and right now yours is uniformly dark on all four sides.

Mechanisms 4, 5 and 6 are yours alone

Missing piece, Sources disagree and Correction cannot be written honestly by a single-outlet account, and a human creator would have to do the comparison by hand for every story. Your pipeline computes this for free and currently discards it. They're also the three most trust-building shapes in the list — which is precisely the position the market has left open. If you add nothing else, add these three.

Bind each mechanism to a layout

Right now the hook rotates but the composition never does, so the variety is invisible. Sources disagree gets a split canvas with two columns. Scale made physical gets a full-bleed roll-up counter. Correction gets a struck-through original figure next to the corrected one. One line in the scene builder, and the account has eight looks instead of one.

The pacing law

Nothing on screen may sit unchanged for more than 2.5 seconds. Taken from Dylan Page, who runs the largest news account on TikTok at roughly 19M followers on exactly this principle — the frame never repeats, so the eye is never given permission to leave. Enforce it as an assertion inside normalise_durations(): any scene longer than 2.5s must contain an internal event.

Every one maps onto an easing function you already have. ease_out_back is sitting unused in the file right now and it's exactly what stamp needs.

Three more fixes

  • Burned-in captions always. Much of the audience watches muted and Instagram's auto-captions are unstyled and unreliable. Set your own at ~64px, two lines max, in the statement zone.
  • Compose a real cover. save_cover() grabs the frame at t=0.62 — a frame built to be passed through at 24fps, not to sit in a grid competing for a tap. Render a dedicated poster.
  • Seamless loop. Match the last 400ms to the first frame.

What to steal, and the trap to avoid

Five accounts, one transferable move each. Plus the one thing you must not copy.

AccountThe mechanicTake
Ground NewsThe bias bar — one proprietary graphic that is the product argument, understood in a glance without marketing copyThe whole §01 idea. They own bias; take corroboration.

The trap

Morning Brew wins on voice — warm, irreverent, unmistakably a person. It's the most copied thing in this sector and it is the one thing you must not attempt. You have nobody behind the account, and a machine performing warmth is worse than no warmth at all.

The research is blunt about the trade: audiences find creators more entertaining, easier to understand and more relatable — and simultaneously less trustworthy and less impartial. You cannot win the first three. The fourth is wide open, trust in news is at a record-low 37%, and 62% of people are actively worried about telling real from fake.

Your voice is restraint and precision. Not personality. Everything else in this document follows from that one decision.

Channel allocation

42% of 18–24s use Instagram for news — the largest platform for that age group. 15% of under-25s use X, and US usage fell 7 points year on year. You're currently spending roughly equal design effort on both. Stop. X gets volume, replies and reused Instagram crops; the saved time goes into reels.

Ignore followers and likes. Both lag, both are gameable, neither predicts installs. A post with 400 saves and 60 likes beats the inverse — and optimising for likes pushes you toward the outrage register that the entire trust position depends on avoiding.

Two reels and one or two carousels a day is roughly 60 assets a month: enough to read one difference, not three. Run a single variable per phase.

On the benchmark numbers floating around

Figures like "60–75% slide-1→2 swipe-through" and "carousels are saved 35% more than single images" come from social-tooling vendors, not Meta — treat them as directional only. The audience statistics cited throughout this document are from the Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026 (48 markets, ~2,000 respondents each) and are citable.
