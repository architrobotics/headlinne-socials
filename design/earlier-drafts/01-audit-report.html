Design Review Headlinne · Instagram · X 15 Aug 2026

The feed is competent. That's the problem.

Nine assets came out of python -m headlinne preview. None of them are embarrassing — the typography is clean, the system is consistent, the copy is tight. They are, however, indistinguishable from every other automated news account, they throw away roughly 40% of every canvas, and they contain no evidence that Headlinne is a product rather than a quote generator. This document audits all nine, studies the accounts that actually win in this sector, and specifies the replacement system.

Seen at thumbnail scale — which is how the algorithm and the audience see them first — the entire output collapses into one texture: dark brown rectangles with a light bar across the middle. Nothing in this grid tells you what any post is about, and nothing distinguishes a reel from a carousel from an ad. That is the whole audit in one image.

01Audit

Twelve specific defects

Ranked by how much each one costs you in the feed. "High" means it is suppressing reach or comprehension right now; "Med" means it is capping how good the work can get.

HIGH
There are no photographs anywhere

Every single asset is type on a gradient. News is a visual medium — a photograph is the fastest possible answer to "what is this about." Your reel renderer already has _photo_plate() and a KenBurns class in render/motion.py; the carousel never calls anything like them. You built the capability and then shipped around it.

FixPhotography becomes the default bed for cover slides and hook scenes. Type sits on a scrim over the image, not on an empty gradient. Where no usable image exists, fall back to a generated data graphic — never to bare gradient.

HIGH
Roughly 40% of the cover slide is empty

On the technology cover, everything above y≈540 is void except the header row. The composition is bottom-weighted with nothing anchoring the top, so the eye lands on nothing, drifts, and scrolls. The reel cover is worse: the bottom ~430px is entirely empty, and that is the region with the most attention in a vertical video.

FixEvery canvas gets a declared zone map with a fill target. No zone may be empty unless it is deliberate negative space bounded by an image edge.

HIGH
The ghost watermarks read as rendering artifacts

The giant faint h and the 01/02 numerals sit at an opacity that is exactly wrong — visible enough to notice, too faint to be intentional. On the geopolitics slide the "02" plus the ghost "h" behind it produce a smudge that reads as a compression fault. Decorative numbering is also lying: those numerals imply a sequence that carries no information the reader needs.

FixDelete both. If you want a numeral it must be committed — large, cropped hard by the canvas edge, at real contrast, and only where the sequence is genuinely meaningful.

HIGH
The palette has no feed presence

#141210 to warm brown at low internal contrast means the post is a dark smudge against Instagram's white chrome. Feed presence is a function of contrast at the boundary of the post, and yours is uniformly dark, so every post looks like the same hole in the grid. The visible radial gradient blob compounds it — it reads as a JPEG artifact rather than lighting.

FixTwo-surface system. A warm paper ground becomes the default (roughly 70% of posts), ink is reserved for breaking news and hero moments. See §05.

HIGH
Reel text sits inside Instagram's UI

"Reuters, BBC +2" lands around y≈1490 on a 1920-tall frame. Instagram's caption block, handle, audio strip and action rail occupy roughly the bottom 400px and the right 90px. Your source attribution — the single most trust-bearing element you have — is being drawn underneath the username.

FixHard safe zone: all content between y=180 and y=1450, nothing within 120px of the right edge. Enforce it as a constant in code, not by eye.

MED
Attention hierarchy is inverted

The loudest object on every slide is the category pill: saturated coral, black text, top right — a position the eye hits second. It carries the least useful information on the canvas. Meanwhile the sources line, which is your actual differentiator, is set in the faintest grey available.

FixDemote category to a small text label in the header lockup. Promote sourcing into a real designed component (§04).

MED
Slides 1 and 2 are structurally identical

Eyebrow → rule → condensed headline → body → footer, repeated. A carousel needs rhythm: the reason people swipe past slide 2 is that slide 2 promises slide 3 will be different. Yours promises more of the same, and the swipe-through rate will show it.

FixA fixed five-beat structure where each slide has a distinct visual archetype. See §06.

MED
Anton is doing every job, and it is the genre's default font

Anton is free, ubiquitous, and the single most-used face on automated news accounts — it signals "template" before a word is read. It also has one weight and one width, so there is no hierarchy available within the display level: every headline shouts at exactly the same volume, and nothing can be emphasised inside a headline.

FixMove to a variable condensed grotesk with real weight and width axes. Add a serif for the interpretive voice — your logo's h is already a serif and nothing in the system picks that thread up.

MED
The reels do not look like video

render/motion.py has ease_out_back, ease_out_quint, Ken Burns, rising lines and a progress bar — real motion primitives. But the design language they animate is a static type card, so the output looks like a slideshow of stills. There is no kinetic type, no word-level reveal, no number that counts, nothing that could only exist in motion.

FixA named motion vocabulary of five transitions plus word-level headline reveal and roll-up counters. Full spec in §06.

MED
The reel cover is a frame grab, not a cover

motion.save_cover() lifts the frame at t=0.62. That frame was composed to be passed through at 24fps, not to sit still in a grid competing for a tap. It has no call, no crop discipline, and inherits whatever mid-animation state it lands in.

FixRender a dedicated cover composition. It is a poster, and it is the only frame most people will ever see.

MED
The X cards are square

1080×1080 in a timeline gets letterboxed or centre-cropped, wasting the horizontal space that is the whole advantage of X's layout. You are also spending equal design effort on X and Instagram when the audience data says you shouldn't (§03).

Fix16:9 at 1200×675 for timeline cards, 4:5 at 1080×1350 when you want vertical dominance. Reuse Instagram artwork rather than designing X separately.

MED
The story card is a document screenshot

Four stops × (label + two lines of body) is roughly 90 words at 1080². The structure — WHAT HAPPENED / HOW WE GOT HERE / WHY IT MATTERS / WHAT TO WATCH — is a genuinely good idea borrowed from the right place. The execution asks someone to read an essay in a feed.

FixKeep the four-stop skeleton, cut to ≤12 words per stop, and let the four stops become a visual timeline rather than four paragraphs.

What is already right

Don't rebuild these: the deterministic hook rotation in generate/hooks.py is a genuinely strong idea and most accounts have nothing like it. The category-colour system is sound. The copy is clean, plain and free of the "and here's why it matters 🧵👇" register that plagues the sector. The four-stop story structure is correct. The offline preview command means you can iterate on design without burning API calls — that is worth more than it sounds.

Grouped by what they can teach you. For each: the design mechanic that does the work, why it works, and the specific transferable move. Not all of them are competitors — the most useful lessons come from adjacent formats.

Tier 1 — Direct product rivals

Ground News
Bias-aware aggregator
Mechanic
The bias bar — a horizontal stacked bar showing how many outlets covering a story lean left, centre and right, plus the "Blindspot" feature showing what one side isn't covering.
Why it works
It is a single proprietary visual primitive that is the product argument. You understand the value proposition in one glance without reading a word of marketing. It is screenshot-able, instantly recognisable out of context, and it makes the person sharing it look discerning rather than partisan.
Distribution
Notably, they barely fight for organic Instagram reach — the growth engine is YouTube sponsor reads, where a creator demonstrates the bar on screen. The visual primitive is what makes a 45-second sponsor read possible.
Take this
Build one proprietary graphic that argues your product. Ground News owns bias. Nobody owns corroboration — and your pipeline already verifies stories across sources. See §04.
Particle
AI news app · Lightspeed-backed
Mechanic
Multi-perspective AI summaries with a "ask the story a question" affordance and spoken personalised feeds.
Why it works
Their positioning leans on speed of catch-up rather than depth. The product demo is the marketing — short screen recordings of a question being asked and answered with citations.
Take this
Your X promo card already says "Ask the news a question, get answers with sources." That is a demo, not a poster. Screen-record the actual interaction and let the product move.

Tier 2 — Design-led news brands

Chartr / Sherwood News
Acquired by Robinhood, 2023
Mechanic
One chart, one insight, one annotation. Light ground, a single saturated accent, an enormous plain-language title above the chart, and one arrow or label pointing at the exact thing you're meant to notice.
Why it works
A chart is evidence, and evidence gets shared because sharing it makes the sharer look informed rather than opinionated. It also survives the thumbnail test — a chart shape is legible at 120px where a paragraph is not. Their stated aim is to make you feel rewarded for the time, which is a different goal from making you feel outraged.
Detail worth copying
The title is never the chart's technical description. It is the conclusion. "Nobody is buying second cars any more" over the chart, not "US household vehicle ownership, 2015–2025."
Take this
Slide 3 of every carousel becomes an evidence slide with a real generated chart. This is the single highest-leverage addition available to you, because it is the one thing on this list that nobody expects an automated account to do.
Axios
Smart Brevity
Mechanic
Fixed structural units with fixed labels — Why it matters, The big picture, Between the lines, What's next — each followed by one or two sentences maximum.
Why it works
The labels are a contract with the reader: you always know what kind of sentence is coming next, so you can enter and exit anywhere without losing the thread. It also makes the writing self-policing, because a label like "Why it matters" makes a vague sentence obviously wrong.
Take this
You already do this in the story card. Push the same labelled units into reel scene structure and carousel slides so the whole system shares one grammar.
Morning Brew
Newsletter → social flywheel
Mechanic
A consistent, warm, slightly irreverent voice applied to business news, with social used as a top-of-funnel that always points back to the newsletter subscription.
Why it works
The voice is the moat — the same story from Reuters and from Brew reads completely differently, and readers choose the one that feels like a person. Crucially they never let a post be purely informational; there is always a stance or a joke that gives you a reason to send it to someone.
Careful here
This is the hardest thing on the list to copy with an automated pipeline, and attempting it badly produces the worst possible output — a machine doing forced banter. Your voice differentiator should be restraint and precision, not personality. See §03.
Visual Capitalist / Voronoi
Infographic publisher
Mechanic
Dense, highly-styled infographics built on public data — government sources, IMF, World Bank — with heavy brand typography and annotation layered over charts generated in Tableau or Flourish.
Why it works
Density is the product. A Visual Capitalist graphic rewards a long look, which drives saves — and saves are the strongest ranking signal available on a static post. They are explicitly optimising for the artefact being kept, not scrolled.
Take this
The pipeline: clean data → generate the chart programmatically → style it with brand typography as a second pass. That is exactly reproducible in Pillow, and it is how your evidence slide should be built.

Tier 3 — Creator-led news

Dylan Page ("News Daddy")
≈19M TikTok · #1 news account
Mechanic
Cold open on the single most arresting fact — no logo, no intro, no "hey guys." Rapid cuts roughly every 1.5–2.5 seconds, B-roll under every sentence, escalation beats ("and it gets worse"), and an exit that hands the argument to the comments.
Why it works
The structure is built entirely around 3-second retention. Face-to-camera plus constant visual change means the frame never repeats, so there is no moment where the eye has permission to leave. His stated approach is quick to start, quick to finish, everything at once.
Take this
The pacing law, not the persona. Nothing on screen may persist unchanged for more than 2.5 seconds. Your current reels hold a static composition for far longer than that.
Cleo Abram — Huge If True
Optimistic tech explainer
Mechanic
Frame a technology question as a genuine open curiosity, then take the viewer along the process of answering it. Deliberately optimistic framing as a counter-position to doom-default tech coverage.
Why it works
She describes it as expressing her own curiosity so people can follow it — the viewer is placed alongside the presenter rather than lectured. The optimism is also a positioning wedge in a category where negativity is the default.
Take this
This is the model for your evergreen educational reel — the one that isn't tied to today's news. Open on a question, not a statement. Note the cost though: she publishes roughly monthly, with hundreds of hours per video. Take the structure, not the production budget.
The News Movement
Social-first news for under-35s
Mechanic
Platform-native, creator-fronted news built for people who will never visit a homepage. Reaches roughly two-thirds of UK 18–35s.
Why it works
The finding worth internalising: their best-performing content is original journalism, not commentary or aggregation. Format-native packaging plus something genuinely new beats format-native packaging alone.
Take this
Aggregation alone will plateau. Your equivalent of "original" is cross-source synthesis that no single outlet published — the thing your ranking and verification layer already computes and then throws away.

What the audience data forces you to do

From the Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026 — 48 markets, ~2,000 respondents each. These numbers should decide your channel allocation, not taste.

Three decisions this makes for you

1 — Instagram is the whole game; X is a side channel

42% of 18–24s using Instagram for news against 15% of under-25s on X is not a close call. You are currently spending roughly equal design effort on both. Stop. X gets volume and replies, not bespoke artwork: reuse the Instagram crop, post more often, and spend the saved time on reels. The −33% collapse in search traffic to news sites is the same story from another angle — the discovery surface has moved into feeds, permanently.

2 — Your automation is a liability on personality and an asset on trust

This is the most important strategic point in this document. Creators are winning because they are more entertaining, easier to understand, more relatable — and you cannot compete on any of those, because there is nobody behind the account. Trying to will produce a machine performing warmth, which is worse than no warmth at all.

But the same research says creators score badly on trust and impartiality, trust in news overall is at a record low of 37%, and 62% of people are actively worried about distinguishing real from fake. That is an open flank. A faceless account that shows its working — every claim sourced, every story corroborated across named outlets, no opinion, no outrage — is occupying the exact position the creator economy has vacated. Your differentiator is evidence, and evidence is a design problem.

3 — Video is non-negotiable, but yours must be watchable

77% weekly online news video, beating broadcast in 45 markets. Two reels a day is the right call. The problem is not cadence, it is that the reels currently look like stills. §06 fixes that.

Caveat on benchmark figures

Widely-quoted carousel benchmarks — 60–75% slide-1→2 swipe-through on a healthy hook, 1.5–3% save rate on educational carousels, carousels saved ~35% more than single images — come from social-tooling vendors, not from Meta. Treat them as directional targets for your own baseline, not as facts. The Reuters figures above are from primary research and can be cited.

04Wedge

Build the corroboration bar

The one recommendation in this document that is strategy rather than craft. Everything in §05 and §06 is worth doing; this is the thing that would make the account recognisable.

Primitive

Ground News owns bias because they built one graphic that says it. Nobody owns corroboration. Your pipeline already ranks and verifies stories across multiple sources — it computes exactly the data that would fill this graphic, and then discards it into a grey "Reuters, BBC +2" caption at the bottom of the slide.

The component: a compact horizontal bar carried by every asset in the system, showing how many independent outlets are reporting the story, how strongly they agree, and which ones. It is not a footer credit. It is a designed information graphic with its own space in the layout.

ElementEncodesSpec
Segment countNumber of independent outlets carrying the story6–14 ticks, 1 per outlet
Segment fillCorroborated / partial / single-sourcesolid / half / outline
Bar colourAgreement strengthgo · neutral · flag
WordmarksThe three highest-weight named outlets14px, tabular
NumeralTotal source count, largedisplay, 44px
TimestampAge of the oldest confirming reportmono, 12px

Why this is the right bet

  • It argues the product without marketing copy. Someone who has never heard of Headlinne understands what the app does from one screenshot.
  • It is defensible. It requires a multi-source verification pipeline to generate honestly. Competitors who fake it will get caught, and competitors who don't have the pipeline can't fake it at all.
  • It solves the empty-canvas problem structurally. Every slide now has a piece of real information design in it, so no slide is ever pure type on a gradient.
  • It is the trust position from §03, made visible. Record-low trust plus 62% fake-news anxiety is a market asking for exactly this.
  • It makes screenshots identifiable. Anyone reposting your card carries the bar with it.

The honesty constraint

This only works if the bar is never decorative. A single-source story must render as a visibly thin, outlined, one-tick bar — and you must be willing to post it that way. The moment the graphic is padded to look impressive it becomes a liability rather than an asset, and it is the kind of thing that gets noticed publicly exactly once.

Two surfaces, not one

The core structural change. Your brand is warm, editorial and terracotta — and you have rendered it as near-black. Paper becomes the default ground; ink is reserved. Practically this gives you a checkerboard grid, so consecutive posts alternate light and dark and the profile stops reading as one continuous smudge.

Note the terracotta shifts between surfaces — #C4562F on paper, #E8763F on ink. A single accent hex cannot hold its perceived weight across both grounds, and this is the most common way two-surface systems fail.

Category colours — keep, but demote

Technology coral, Finance emerald, Geopolitics amber all stay. They stop being pill backgrounds and become a 4px rule under the header lockup, plus the accent on the corroboration bar. Same information, a tenth of the volume.

Pillow variable-font constraint — check before committing

Variable axes need ImageFont.FreeTypeFont.set_variation_by_axes(), which requires a FreeType build with variation support. You already ship two variable fonts, so it is probably fine — but verify with a one-line probe before designing around the width axis. If it isn't available, ship static instances of the three widths you actually use instead.

The pacing law

Nothing on screen may persist unchanged for more than 2.5 seconds. This is the single rule taken from Dylan Page, and it is enforceable in code: if a scene's duration exceeds 2.5s it must contain an internal event — a word revealing, a number ticking, a photograph pushing, a rule wiping. normalise_durations() already exists; add the assertion there.

Also

  • Burned-in captions, always. A large share of viewing is muted, and Instagram's auto-captions are unstyled and unreliable. Set them in your own type at ~64px, two lines maximum, centred in the statement zone.

If a story genuinely needs more than 48 words it is a carousel, not a card — enforce that as a branch in code rather than by shrinking the type.

X — generate/twitter.py, render/card.py

Given 15% of under-25s and a 7pp decline, X earns the least design investment on this list. Change the aspect ratio, reuse Instagram artwork, and spend the time elsewhere.

  • 1200×675 (16:9) for timeline cards; 1080×1350 when you want vertical dominance.
  • Text first, image second. On X the post text is the hook and the image is the evidence. Your card currently duplicates the headline in both, which wastes the strongest position you have.
  • Thread the corroboration. Post the claim, then reply with the sources as a second post. It reads as showing your working and it doubles your surface area.
  • Kill the promo card format. A static poster saying "Ask the news a question" converts far worse than a six-second screen recording of it happening.
07Hooks

Extending the hook rotation

generate/hooks.py is the strongest file in the repository. The architecture — code owns the rhetorical shape, the model fills it, rotation is deterministic — is right. It needs more shapes and a visual dimension.

Archetypes
ArchetypeShapeOpens withStatus
ContradictionName the assumption, puncture it"Everyone assumed this would raise prices. It did the opposite."Have

The last two are yours alone

Correction and Disagreement are hooks that only a multi-source verification pipeline can generate honestly — a single-outlet account cannot write them, and a creator would have to do the work manually. They also happen to be the most trust-building content in the entire list, which is exactly the position §03 says you should be occupying. If you add nothing else from this table, add these two.

Give each archetype a visual treatment

Right now the hook varies but the layout never does, so the rotation is invisible to the eye. Bind each archetype to a composition — contradiction gets the split paper/ink canvas, scale shift gets the full-bleed count animation, disagreement gets a two-column source comparison. One line in the scene builder, and suddenly the account has eight looks instead of one.

08Build

Sequenced so the feed improves from week one

Ordered by visible improvement per unit of work, not by architectural tidiness. Each phase ships something you can look at in python -m headlinne preview.

Phases
Phase 1 — Stop the bleeding
Half a day · pure deletion and constants
  • Delete the ghost h watermark and the 01/02 numerals.
  • Delete the pagination dots and the SWIPE pill.
  • Move Instagram statics from 1080×1080 to 1080×1350.
  • Move X cards to 1200×675.
  • Add the reel safe-zone constants and assert nothing renders below y=1450.
  • Demote the category pill to a rule under the header lockup.
Phase 2 — Photography
Two days · news/images.py, render/carousel.py
  • Wire the existing image loader into the carousel cover and slide 2.
  • Build the scrim system — a gradient that guarantees text contrast over any photograph, tested against a light image and a dark one.
  • Fallback chain: article image → category stock → generated data graphic. Never bare gradient.
Phase 3 — The corroboration bar
Three days · new render/sourcebar.py
  • Surface the source data your ranking layer already computes.
  • Render at three sizes: inline (carousel footer), full (close slide), animated (reel payoff).
  • Honest degradation for single-source stories — build and test that case first, so it can never be quietly skipped.
Phase 4 — Two-surface palette and type
Two days · config.py, render/theme.py
  • Add the paper surface and the per-surface accent shift.
  • Alternate surfaces by slot so the grid checkerboards.
  • Swap Anton for a variable condensed grotesk; probe Pillow variation support first.
  • Add the serif for interpretive copy and the mono for data.
Phase 5 — Motion language
Four days · render/motion.py, render/reel.py
  • Implement the five named transitions against the existing easing functions.
  • Enforce the 2.5-second pacing law in normalise_durations().
  • Word-level headline reveal and roll-up counters.
  • Dedicated cover composition, replacing the t=0.62 frame grab.
  • Seamless loop on the final 400ms.
Phase 6 — Evidence slide
Four days · new render/chart.py
  • Three chart types only, done well: single-series line, ranked bar, share-of-total.
  • Conclusion-as-title, one annotation, brand typography applied as a second pass.
  • Gate on data availability — skip the slide rather than render a weak chart.
Phase 7 — Hook expansion
Two days · generate/hooks.py
  • Add the six new archetypes, starting with Correction and Disagreement.
  • Bind each archetype to a composition in the scene builder.

Ignore

Follower count and like count. Both are lagging, both are gameable, and neither predicts installs. A post with 400 saves and 60 likes is worth more to you than the inverse, and optimising for likes will actively push you toward the outrage register that your trust position depends on avoiding.

Test one thing per phase

Two reels and one or two carousels a day is roughly 60 assets a month — enough to read a difference, not enough to read three at once. Run one variable per phase: Phase 2 tests photography against gradient, Phase 4 tests paper against ink, Phase 6 tests the evidence slide against a fourth text slide. Anything more granular is noise at this volume.
